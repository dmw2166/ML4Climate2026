The final substantive week of the course steps back from method and asks what these
tools are doing in the world. This matters more in climate work than in most fields,
because the technology being deployed to study the problem has become a
non-trivial contributor to it.

## Planned topics

- Large language models: what they are, where they are useful in scientific work,
  and where they fail in ways that are hard to detect
- Agentic AI, and what changes when a model takes actions rather than producing text
- The energy and water footprint of training and inference
- Data centers: siting, grid impact, and the growing tension between AI demand and
  decarbonization timelines
- Who benefits and who bears the cost: access to compute, and the geography of both
  climate impacts and AI infrastructure
- Reproducibility and scientific integrity when models are large, expensive, and
  not fully open
- Policy levers, and what scientists are positioned to influence
